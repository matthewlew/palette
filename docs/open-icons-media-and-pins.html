The media pair, and the two pins

Both changes came from the same mistake on my part: I drew from first principles where the reference set already had an answer.

Pause is not two bars. The reference draws two containers — outer 7×18 at 3.5..10.5 and 13.5..20.5, corner radius 2.5, wall exactly 2, which is our stroke. On the centreline that is two 5×16 boxes. My version was two round-capped bars on a 9..15 ink box: a third of play-fill's mass, so in a single play/pause button the paused state read as the disabled state.

The gap between the two boxes is 3, or 1.5 W. That sits above the stroke-to-stroke floor rather than on it, and it has to: at the floor the two boxes fuse into one slab by 16px.

Play had to be recovered, not read. The reference ships expanded outlines, so the drawing is already the silhouette and the triangle behind it is implied. Extending the straight run either side of each corner and intersecting gives the inner contour as (7,4) (20,12) (7,20) — round numbers, a wall of exactly 2, and one radius of 2.5 on all three vertices. My triangle was (19,12) (7,19) (7,5) with a 1.6 corner: smaller, and far less rounded.

One wrinkle worth recording: the reference rounds all three vertices to the same radius, and ngon rounds to a constant extent. Extent = r / tan(½ θ), and the three interior angles are 58.4°, 63.2° and 58.4°, so a single extent of 0.65 W lands the radii at 1.45 / 1.60 / 1.45 against a target of 1.5. Under a tenth of a unit.

The reference set has no push pin at all — only location-pin-enabled-line and friends — so this one is designed rather than measured, and the brief is entirely about telling the two apart.

They share nothing. location is a balloon with a hole, tapering to a point: a teardrop. pin is a flat wide cap over a solid needle: a T. Not a tilted version of the same object, not the same head at a different angle — a different silhouette, so it survives at 16px without a label.

The cap is a capsule, not a rounded box: a thumb pad is something you press, and fully round ends say that where a 1.6 corner does not.

Its proportions were picked by drawing six and looking, not by reasoning. The obvious first guess — a wide flat cap, 12×4 — reads as a tee; a nearly round one drifts back toward location; and adding the collar a real thumbtack has turns the whole thing into a dagger. At 9×5 the cap's interior is 5 − W = 3, which is 1.5 W rather than sitting on the counter floor, and that spare half-stroke is what keeps it reading as hollow at 16px.

The needle is solid. Outlined it would be 3 wide with a 1-unit hole running down it, and a hole that thin does not read as a counter, it reads as a printing fault. It is also what the set already does for the mark inside location. Its base sits on the cap's centreline, so the top unit is buried in the cap's own wall and the two join without a seam.

The rename is the point. A map marker answers where; pinning is something you do. One name cannot carry both, and Palette's button says “Pin this position”. So the marker took the reference set's own word, location, and pin went to the verb.
