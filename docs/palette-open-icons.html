Palette, on Open Icons

Twenty hand-drawn SVGs replaced by eighteen glyphs from the set. The complaint was that they looked funky and unrelated, and the measurements say why: six sizes and five stroke widths across twenty icons, three different corner treatments, and one glyph drawn on a 16 grid while the rest used 24.

Three icons the app needed and the set did not have

grid-masonry

Two columns of unequal cells, staggered. Same 5..19 box and the same pitch as grid; the split between the two cells falls in a different place on each side.

The short cell is exactly 2W — the smallest a container can be and still keep a counter one stroke wide. Any denser stagger closes.

grid-dense

Nine cells to grid's four. Nine outlined cells cannot hold their counters at this stroke: three cells across a 14-unit span leaves a centreline side of 2, and a 2-unit box at stroke 2 has no hole at all.

So the dense grid is solid marks — the same answer more-horizontal gives. Side 2W with a gap of W lands the outer ink on 4..20, which is grid's silhouette exactly.

pin

A push pin: a capsule cap over a solid needle. The set had only the map marker, which the app had been borrowing — but a marker answers where, and pinning is something you do.

So the marker took the reference set's own word, location, and pin went to the verb. The two share no part of a silhouette: a teardrop against a T. Proportions were picked by drawing six and looking, not by reasoning — the obvious wide flat cap reads as a tee.

Left open

Two icons still sized by CSS

The search glyph and the swatch tick are positioned by their stylesheets against an input and a swatch, so their boxes answer to those, not to the scale. Both pass size={null} and are commented as such.

The tick is the one that matters: it sits in a 14px black disc, which means a 24-grid glyph at 14px. It wants the 16px master.

The 16px master

Every glyph here is the 24 drawing scaled down. The reference set's own 16 is not a scaled 24 — full-bleed container, a 1-unit margin, a glyph box at 62% rather than 58%. Palette renders four icons below 16px today.

LDS's own sprite

lds.css ships 23 placeholder icons and a .lds-icon class that sets stroke-width from --icon-weight. Do not put that class on these glyphs: their mask strokes run 5–6 units wide to cut a moat, and forcing every stroke to 2 would fill the moats back in.

The sprite is the next thing Open Icons should replace, and then the class can go.
